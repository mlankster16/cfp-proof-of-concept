About this call

The Duke Center for Teaching and Learning (CTL) invites Duke faculty to propose new Coursera Specializations in AI and other topics where Duke and Coursera see strong learner demand. The call puts into practice priorities from the AI at Duke Steering Committee Report (February 2026).

Specializations developed through this call are meant to:

  • Support AI curriculum that can be reused in Duke degree programs
  • Give Duke students and alumni practical, career-relevant knowledge and skills
  • Reach learners in the U.S. and around the world, from working professionals to employers looking for AI education
  • Share Duke’s expertise in AI with a global audience

We expect to select at least two Specializations for development beginning in 2027, and to identify a shortlist of two to three more for development in 2028.

Topics we’re looking for

Proposals are reviewed in priority order, starting with Priority 1.

Priority 1

Responsible and sustainable AI

One three-Course Specialization on responsible AI, with one Course focused on sustainable AI, green AI, or sustainable governance of AI. This is the highest priority for this call.

$45,000one Specialization
Priority 2

Coursera high-demand topics

Coursera has identified 25 topics across five categories where learner demand is high. Proposals in this track should name the topic they align with. See the high-demand topics list.

$40,000per Specialization
Priority 3

Role-specific AI competencies

Specializations on how AI applies within a professional field. Proposals for any field are welcome. Job market data shows especially strong demand in these ten:

  • Product and project management
  • Data science and data engineering
  • Marketing
  • Cybersecurity
  • Business analysis
  • Operations management
  • Law
  • Communications
  • Finance and accounting
  • Healthcare, pharmaceuticals, and insurance
$30,000per Specialization

Proposals should aim for content that lasts

AI tools change quickly. Strong proposals teach skills, concepts, and frameworks that learners can keep using as specific tools, apps, and model versions change. No course has an unlimited shelf life, but we’ll give preference to proposals designed with this in mind.

What a Specialization in this call includes

A Coursera Specialization is a series of related Courses that builds toward a larger goal. Learners who complete all three Courses earn a Specialization certificate.

3 Coursesrelated to each other, each with its own focus
2 to 8 hoursof learner time per Course, including videos, activities, and assessments
2 to 4 objectivesper Course, describing what learners will be able to do

A typical Course includes about eight or more videos of 5 to 10 minutes each, four or more practice activities, four or more readings or resources, and four auto-graded quizzes. These are planning guidelines, and your Learning Experience Designer will help you find the right mix for your Course.

Before applying, we recommend spending some time in an existing Coursera course to see how learners experience it: example Duke Coursera Specialization.

How Coursera Courses differ from Duke courses

Coursera learners work on their own schedule, come from a wide range of backgrounds and countries, and usually don’t interact with the instructor directly. Courses are much shorter than a semester, and they’re organized around what learners will practice or produce. One way to picture the scale:

In a Duke courseIn a Coursera SpecializationLearner time
A full semester courseA SpecializationAbout 6 to 24 hours total
A unit or themeA Course2 to 8 hours
A topic within a unitA Module30 minutes to 2 hours
A lecture, reading, or assignmentA video, reading, activity, or quizA few minutes each

What you’d commit to

Development takes 9 to 12 months

Development begins in mid-Spring 2027. Specializations typically launch on Coursera about a week after the final build is submitted. [Add expected launch range, for example late 2027 to early 2028]

Plan on about 60 to 90 hours per Course

We estimate each Course’s faculty lead will spend about 60 to 90 hours across the development window, or roughly 1.5 to 2.5 hours a week. Most of that time goes to:

  • Planning meetings with your Learning Experience Designer
  • Drafting video scripts and slides
  • Recording videos in the studio with a Media Producer
  • Drafting activities, quizzes, and readings
  • Reviewing edited videos and the finished Course

Hours vary with how much material you already have. If multiple faculty share a Specialization, each Course lead carries the hours for their own Course.

How you and CTL share the work

As faculty, you will
  • Set the learning objectives for your Course
  • Draft and revise scripts, slides, readings, activities, and quizzes
  • Record your videos in the studio
  • Review feedback and make content decisions
  • Make sure readings and visuals are open-licensed or used with permission
  • Review and approve your Course before launch
The CTL team will
  • Help plan the Specialization, Course, and Module structure
  • Give guidance on learning design, accessibility, and copyright
  • Design visuals and produce and edit your videos
  • Build and review the Courses in Coursera
  • Prepare landing pages and marketing for launch
  • Respond to learner support needs after launch

Team members respond to requests for feedback within five business days, unless another date is set. Delays on either side can shift the project timeline.

What you need to apply

Materials you already have

Proposals should build on materials you already have. We think about these in two groups, and the proposal form asks about each:

Materials that show your expertise

Published research, books, talks, syllabi, or courses you’ve taught. These show that the Specialization grows out of work you know well.

Materials learners would use directly

Recorded videos, slides, curated readings, activities, and quizzes. These are especially helpful if you’ve already taught with them.

Some projects may move faster

Specializations in this call are planned on a 9 to 12 month timeline. If you already have learner-facing materials you’ve taught with, describe them in your proposal. During shortlist conversations, we’ll look at those materials with you and discuss whether a shorter timeline is realistic.

How we’ll review proposals

The CTL review team reviews proposals against the criteria below. Proposals need to meet most or all of them to move forward.

Required
  • Specialization structure. Three related Courses, each with 2 to 8 hours of learner time and 2 to 4 learning objectives.
  • Topic alignment. The proposal clearly fits one of the three priority tracks.
  • Existing materials. You have teaching, writing, research, or other materials that can be adapted.
Strongly considered
  • Target audience. The proposal names a primary learner audience and explains how the Specialization meets their needs.
  • Instructor readiness. You’ve reviewed a Coursera course and the responsibilities on this page, or have developed a Coursera course with CTL before.
  • Availability. You can commit 9 to 12 months beginning mid-Spring 2027, and the hours described above.
  • Feasibility. The design uses activities and assessments that work well in Coursera, has few outside approvals or dependencies, and has one lead instructor per Course where possible.
  • Clear objectives and vision. Each Course names what learners will be able to do, practice, or produce, with a preference for skills that stay useful as AI changes.
Considered
  • Planned reuse at Duke. You and your school plan to reuse the materials in a Duke degree program.

Proposals that meet the criteria are then reviewed by priority track, starting with Priority 1. Within a track, proposals are compared on how well they meet the criteria and on projected learner demand. Final selections may also take development capacity into account.

Incentives and ownership

The incentive is an advance on future revenue

Each selected Specialization receives an incentive of $45,000 (Priority 1), $40,000 (Priority 2), or $30,000 (Priority 3). The incentive is paid as an advance. Duke first applies Specialization revenue to recover the incentive, and then shares the remaining revenue with instructors. Revenue share details are discussed with shortlisted faculty.

Half of the incentive is paid when the project begins, and the other half when the project is complete. [How incentives are divided among multiple instructors]

You own the materials you create

Course materials developed through this call follow the Duke Faculty Handbook, Appendix M. Under that policy, course authors own the rights to the materials they develop unless they’ve agreed to different terms with their school, and they’re free to reuse those materials in their Duke teaching and other work.
